## 1. Purpose

User-facing long copy authored from the specs in the
[Content Catalogue](15-content-catalogue.md): lesson text for the 15 seed
skills (§7 there), stage descriptions and next-stage previews (§4 there), and
the shared safety/transition copy. This copy loads into the content tables as
version 1 of each item and follows the voice rules in the design system
(calm, plain, reward-based, never shaming, never clinical).

Inherits the catalogue's safety boundary: private-MVP use only until
professionally reviewed; nothing here is veterinary advice, and the app must
render the review-status badge wherever this content appears.

## 2. Shared copy blocks

- **Socialization caution (every socialization surface):** "Ask your vet
  where {name} can go before their vaccinations are complete. Watching from a
  distance and being carried both count as great socialization."
- **Progress reminder (training surfaces):** "Progress states are your own
  read on how it's going — there's no test and no deadline."
- **Session sizing (lesson footer):** "Puppies learn in bursts. A couple of
  minutes is a real session — stop while it's still fun."
- **Homecoming transition (one-time, homecoming day):**
  "{name} is home! 🏠 Want to set up your daily routine? You can change any
  of it later." Buttons: "Set up routine" / "Later".

## 4. Lessons

Format per lesson: promise · steps · watch for · going well. Placeholders:
{name} = pet name. Steps render numbered; "watch for" renders under Common
mistakes.

### skill.marker_intro — Marker word ("yes!")

**Promise:** One little word that tells {name} exactly which moment earned
the treat.

1. Pick one short word the whole household will use — "yes!" works.
2. Say it once, then hand {name} a treat. Word first, treat after.
3. Repeat 5–8 times. That's the whole session.
4. Over the next days, say it the instant {name} does something you like,
   then treat.

**Watch for:** Marking late (the word should catch the exact moment) ·
reaching for the treat before you've said the word.
**Going well:** {name}'s ears perk when they hear it — the word now means
"that! do that again."

### skill.name_response — Name response

**Promise:** {name} hears their name and thinks "something good is about to
happen."

1. Wait until {name} isn't looking at you.
2. Say their name once, brightly.
3. The instant they turn toward you, mark and treat.
4. If they don't turn, don't repeat the name — make a small fun noise
   instead, and make it easier next time.
5. Practice in different rooms, then with mild distractions.

**Watch for:** Repeating the name ("Maple. Maple! MAPLE.") teaches that the
first call doesn't count · never use the name for scolding.
**Going well:** A quick head-turn on the first call, most of the time, in
easy places.

### skill.sit — Sit

**Promise:** A polite default that beats jumping every time.

1. Hold a treat at {name}'s nose, then move it slowly up and slightly back.
2. As the head comes up, the bottom goes down. The moment it touches — mark
   and treat.
3. After a few successes, try the same hand motion without a treat in the
   hand; treat from the other hand.
4. Add the word "sit" just before the motion once the movement is reliable.

**Watch for:** Luring forever (fade the treat from the guiding hand early) ·
never push the rear down — let {name} figure it out.
**Going well:** {name} sits on the word or a small hand signal, no treat in
sight until after.

### skill.hand_target — Hand target ("touch")

**Promise:** A friendly "come here and check in" that works anywhere.

1. Present a flat palm a hand-width from {name}'s nose.
2. Puppies are curious — when the nose boops the palm, mark and treat.
3. Move the palm to new spots: lower, higher, left, right.
4. Add the word "touch" just before presenting the hand.

**Watch for:** Moving your hand toward {name} (let them come to the hand) ·
sessions longer than a minute or two.
**Going well:** {name} crosses the room to boop your palm.

### skill.recall_foundations — Recall ("come")

**Promise:** The one that matters most: {name} comes when called, happily.

1. Start two steps away in a quiet room. Say "{name}, come!" once, warmly.
2. The instant they move toward you, celebrate — mark, treat generously,
   make it a party.
3. Release them to go back to whatever they were doing. Coming to you
   shouldn't end the fun.
4. Gradually add distance, then new rooms, then the garden.
5. Sometimes grab the collar gently before treating — so a reach isn't
   scary later.

**Watch for:** Calling {name} for things they dislike (baths, ending play) —
go and get them instead · repeating the cue · staying at one difficulty
forever, or jumping to the park too soon.
**Going well:** A fast, happy turn-and-run to you in easy places, every time.

### skill.leave_it — Leave it

**Promise:** {name} chooses to look away from tempting things — because it
pays better.

1. Close a boring treat in your fist and let {name} investigate.
2. Wait out the licking and pawing. The instant they pull away — mark and
   treat *from the other hand*.
3. Repeat until they pull back from the fist immediately.
4. Move the closed hand to the floor; same game.
5. Add the words "leave it" just before presenting, once the pull-away is
   quick.

**Watch for:** Escalating too fast (an open hand or dropped food is
graduate-level) · snatching the item away — the point is {name} choosing.
**Going well:** {name} sees the fist, looks at you instead.

### skill.drop_it — Drop it (trade)

**Promise:** {name} gives things up willingly, because trading always pays.

1. While {name} holds a toy, offer a treat near their nose.
2. When the toy drops — mark, treat, and *give the toy right back* when
   it's safe to.
3. Add the words "drop it" just before the offer.
4. Practice with different toys; keep the trade generous.

**Watch for:** Chasing {name} to get the item (that's the best game ever —
don't start it) · always trading down (losing the toy forever teaches
{name} to guard it).
**Going well:** The toy drops on the words, tail still wagging.

### skill.crate_comfort — Crate comfort

**Promise:** The crate becomes {name}'s own calm room, not a cage.

1. Set the crate up cozy, door open. Drop treats inside and walk away — no
   pressure.
2. Feed meals just inside the door, then further in.
3. When {name} settles inside on their own, calmly drop a treat in.
4. Close the door for a few seconds while treating, open before any fuss.
5. Build duration in small steps, always ending while it's easy.

**Watch for:** Using the crate for time-outs · rushing duration — one bad
scare undoes a week of good work.
**Going well:** {name} wanders in for naps with the door open.

### skill.settle_on_mat — Settle on a mat

**Promise:** An off-switch: {name} learns that the mat means relax.

1. Put a mat down; treat {name} for any interest in it.
2. Treat for paws on, then for sitting, then for lying down — at {name}'s
   pace.
3. Feed calm: slow, quiet treats between the paws while they're lying down.
4. Take a step away and return before {name} gets up. Build slowly.
5. Practice while you sit nearby with a cup of tea — boring is the point.

**Watch for:** Only practicing when {name} is already sleepy (fine to
start, but practice mildly awake too) · expecting long holds in week one.
**Going well:** {name} sees the mat come out and flops onto it.

### skill.alone_time — Alone-time foundations

**Promise:** {name} learns that being alone is safe and short-lived — the
foundation against separation stress.

1. Start with {name} settled (mat or crate). Step out of sight for two
   seconds, return calmly.
2. Return *before* any fussing — you're teaching "you always come back,
   it's fine."
3. Vary it: sometimes two seconds, sometimes ten. Keep returns boring.
4. Build toward leaving the room during normal life — kettle on, coat on
   and off again.
5. Work up to real short absences only when {name} is relaxed at every
   step.

**Watch for:** Sneaking out (let departures be visible and unremarkable) ·
jumping from one minute to one hour.
**Going well:** {name} glances up when you leave the room, then goes back
to their chew.

### skill.paw_handling — Paw handling

**Promise:** Nail trims and muddy-paw wipes without a wrestling match.

1. Touch a paw for one second, mark, treat. That's it.
2. Progress to holding briefly, then to gently pressing a toe.
3. Pair every touch with something good; stop before {name} pulls away.
4. Later, touch paws with a towel, then show the nail clippers nearby —
   no clipping yet, just good associations.

**Watch for:** Gripping tightly when {name} pulls back (release on calm,
not on struggle — better yet, go slower) · pushing past discomfort.
**Going well:** {name} offers a paw and leaves it in your hand.

### skill.body_handling — Ears, mouth, body handling

**Promise:** Vet visits and grooming feel like old news, because gentle
hands are normal.

1. In a calm moment, touch an ear for a second — mark, treat.
2. Rotate areas over sessions: ears, lips (lift briefly), collar area,
   tail, belly.
3. Keep sessions under two minutes, always ending on an easy touch.
4. Add gentle restraint last: a soft hold for one second, treat, release.

**Watch for:** Long sessions · handling without pairing rewards (touch
should predict good things, not just happen).
**Going well:** {name} leans in for the exam-style once-over.

### skill.harness_intro — Collar/harness introduction

**Promise:** The harness predicts adventures, not restraint.

1. Show the harness; treat {name} for sniffing it.
2. Feed treats through the head opening so {name} pokes their own head in.
3. Clip it on loosely for a few seconds while treating, then off.
4. Build up wearing time during pleasant things — meals, play.

**Watch for:** Forcing it over the head · leaving it on while {name} is
distressed — take a step back instead.
**Going well:** {name} sees the harness and comes over.

### skill.loose_leash — Loose-leash foundations

**Promise:** Walks where the leash stays soft — starting in the hallway,
not the high street.

1. Indoors, clip the leash on and just walk around treating {name} for
   being near your leg.
2. When the leash goes tight, stop being interesting: stand still. The
   moment it slackens, mark, treat, walk on.
3. Change direction often — following you becomes the game.
4. Take it to the garden, then quiet outdoor spots, keeping sessions
   short.

**Watch for:** Letting pulling work sometimes (if tight leash ever gets
{name} where they want, pulling pays) · sessions long enough to get
frustrating for either of you.
**Going well:** Slack-leash steps beside you in easyish places, {name}
checking in.

### skill.shake — Shake / give paw

**Promise:** A party trick that doubles as paw-handling practice.

1. Hold a treat in a loose fist at {name}'s chest height.
2. Most puppies paw at the fist — the instant a paw lifts, mark and treat.
3. Add an open palm as the target; treat for a paw landing in it.
4. Add the word "shake" just before offering the palm.

**Watch for:** Grabbing the paw instead of letting {name} offer it.
**Going well:** Paw in palm on the word — cue applause from the household.
